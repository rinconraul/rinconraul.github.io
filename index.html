Welcome to the Adaptive Socio-Physical Infrastructure Lab

We study infrastructure as a socio-physical system: its behavior emerges from the interplay of users, infrastructure owners, components, and environmental stressors over time.

The Adaptive Socio-Physical Infrastructure Lab at the University of Colorado Boulder develops computational and probabilistic methods to evaluate, design, and manage infrastructure systems exposed to deterioration, natural hazards, and climate stressors. These methods show how damage to physical systems reaches the people who depend on them, and help decision-makers choose interventions that keep urban infrastructure safe, resilient, and fair, now and into the future.

The lab is led by Raul Rincon, who joins the Department of Civil, Environmental, and Architectural Engineering as Assistant Professor in Spring 2027.

Our vision

Resilience is often treated as a single sequence: a hazard strikes, the system responds, and the community recovers. We take a systemic view instead. Societies, infrastructure, and the environment shape one another continuously, and safe, thriving communities depend on understanding those interactions as a whole.

What we aim for

  • Modeling socio-physical infrastructure as multiscale systems to understand interactions that determine network and community-scale performance.
  • Informing decision-making to improve infrastructure resilience, while mitigating the effects of uncertainty our own modeling choices introduce.
  • Adaptive infrastructure modeling and management that accounts for how users, physical systems, and stressors change together over a system’s lifetime.
Research

From Uncertain Data and Models to Adaptive Socio-Physical Infrastructure Systems

Thrust 1

Multiscale Infrastructure Systems Modeling

We ask how to make structure-to-network scale reliability, risk, and resilience assessments computationally feasible without sacrificing the realism of the individual submodels involved in the analysis.

See more details and references
Thrust 2

Uncertainty Quantification and Uncertainties in the Modeling Process

Quantifying not just parameter uncertainty but the hidden uncertainty of modeling choices, and how it compounds across scales and communities.

See more details and references
Thrust 3

Dynamic User-Physical Infrastructure Systems

How users, owners, physical networks, and environmental stressors interact and evolve. These interactions and their hidden relationships shape long-term resilience and sustainability.

  • Aug 2026 Report

    The Learning From Earthquakes Virtual Earthquake Reconnaissance Team (VERT) report on the M7.4 San José del Palmar, Colombia earthquake (August 10, 2026) has been released. The report documents publicly available data on the earthquake and its impacts within a week of the event. So far, structural collapses, structural damages, and widespread non-structural damage across critical facilities, housing, and infrastructure have led to many facilities and infrastructure components losing functionality. These impacts are a sobering reminder of the continued need for coordinated efforts across academia, industry, and policy toward resilient cities. Read the report here.

  • Aug 2026 Field

    On August 10, 2026, a M7.4 earthquake struck near San José del Palmar, Colombia, causing widespread infrastructure damage, hundreds of fatalities, and thousands of injuries. Raul participated in post-earthquake field reconnaissance in the days following the event, supporting structural safety inspections in the affected region. We send our condolences to all those affected and to the millions whose family and friends suffered any loss.

    New collaborative preprint: “Phase Transitions in Collective Damage of Civil Structures under Natural Hazards” — joint work with Sebin Oh, Jinyan Zhao, Jamie E. Padgett, and Ziqi Wang. We show that urban structural damage exhibits phase-transition phenomena analogous to first- and second-order transitions in statistical physics, with implications for how engineering modeling practices bias urban risk metrics. Available on arXiv.
