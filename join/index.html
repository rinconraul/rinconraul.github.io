Open PhD position

Updated November 2026

I am recruiting one PhD student for Fall 2027 to join the Adaptive Socio-Physical Infrastructure Lab in Civil, Environmental, and Architectural Engineering at CU Boulder.

Topics include:

  • Reliability, risk, and resilience modeling, from single structures to infrastructure systems
  • Uncertainty quantification across scales, including the uncertainty introduced by modeling choices
  • Graph-theoretic, multifidelity, and machine learning methods for socio-physical infrastructure and natural hazards
  • Smart and adaptive systems that update data, models, and decisions over time
  • Informing decision-makers and policymakers to support long-term resilience and sustainability

To be considered:

  1. Apply to the CEAE PhD program by Dec. 1 (international applicants) or Dec. 15 (domestic applicants).
  2. Email me at raul.rincon@colorado.edu with the subject “PhD Fall 2027 - LastName”, your CV, and a short note on one or two research questions you would like to work on.

I read every email that follows these steps, but I may not be able to reply to each one.

What I look for in PhD students

If you are drawn to problems at the intersection of modeling engineered systems, multiscale uncertainty quantification, objectively informing decision-making, and mitigating societal impacts from infrastructure failures, I would like to hear from you. Strong candidates typically have some combination of:

  • A background in civil/structural engineering, industrial engineering, systems engineering, statistics, applied mechanics, applied physics, or a closely related quantitative field
  • Comfort with, or a genuine interest in developing, probabilistic and computational skills, and involvement in analyzing infrastructure systems at multiple scales
  • Intellectual curiosity about how engineering models connect to real decisions about infrastructure and communities
  • Ability to engage in computational engineering research, using field observations, surveys, multi-modal data, and laboratory data to build and test models
  • Prior research experience (undergraduate thesis, co-authorship, lab work) is valued

A master’s degree is desirable; undergraduate applicants with research experience are also encouraged.

What working in the lab looks like

In the Adaptive Socio-Physical Infrastructure Lab, you can expect:

  • Substantive, regular mentoring
  • Exposure to the full research lifecycle
  • Professional skills development
  • Honest career conversations

This is a collegial, intellectually serious environment where new ideas are welcomed and existing ones are questioned respectfully.

Visiting students and scholars

Visiting students and scholars with their own funding are welcome. Please email me at raul.rincon@colorado.edu with your CV, a short description of the research you would like to pursue, your proposed dates, and your funding source.
